## The concrete problem

Three reports in mattpocock/skills described failures in the same planning workflow:

| Report | Created, UTC | Reported problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [#1398 — lost context](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/1398) | Nov 8, 13:58:32 | Reused question numbers made follow-up answers ambiguous; the reporter wanted stable references and original question context. |
| [#1401 — recommendation built on a pending fact](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/1401) | Nov 8, 23:13:37 | Recommendations relied on codebase facts still being investigated, then had to be retracted. |
| [#1402 — confirmation that adds new facts](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/1402) | Nov 8, 23:13:42 | New reference material arrived with a confirmation and changed previously settled decisions. |

These are three reports from **two people**, not three independent samples of a large market. At observation they were open, with no comments or reactions. Their claims were not independently reproduced against the original agent sessions.

The inference is modest: a person should be able to preserve question identity, declare an answer's dependencies, and see which acknowledgements no longer match recorded evidence. QuestionLedger implements that workflow. It does not interpret a conversation or decide whether new information is relevant.

## Prior art and overlap

These primary READMEs were reviewed. Links pin the observed repository revision.

- [Code Recall](https://github.com/erikhuang76821/code-recall/blob/d8fd1dd7d93961f85be310a902e74c4ab28629ef/README.md) already provides local decision/lesson ledgers, lifecycle management and agent-context hooks.
- [Plan Tree](https://github.com/SeemSeam/plan-tree/blob/b57be82610307fba9a1c266b6e7e2f35dd5b7e33/README.md) already preserves stable planning IDs, open questions, evidence and history.
- [Scrutable](https://github.com/MSKazemi/scrutable/blob/7b0c3d632fd47f91c4384e2ec7e20d871cac969d/README.md) is the closest conceptual overlap: it describes question/evidence ledgers, dependencies, reopening when evidence changes, and a structural validator. Its text-first framework is much broader; interactive navigation is optional future work.
- [Log4brains](https://github.com/thomvaill/log4brains/blob/37187fc8e0cd615c155eb27d6116e1e05681a918/README.md) offers a searchable static ADR knowledge base, CLI creation and local preview.
- [ADR Tools](https://github.com/npryce/adr-tools/blob/b3279baf9be2207d1a4f4bbd608fd0b591c72aee/README.md) supplies numbered Markdown decisions and supersession.
- [adr-skills](https://github.com/wooxogh/adr-mcp-setup/blob/f2c42dd6d92b60c59897d85fc4fa947f1b30faa9/README.md) captures conversations into ADRs, links decisions and exposes age-based stale alerts.

This is a README-level comparison, not a benchmark or exhaustive survey. No claim is made that QuestionLedger invents stable references, decision graphs, local ledgers or evidence-driven reopening.

## Scope chosen for version 0.1

The first version combines stable question ancestry, manually declared facts, draft/review states, deterministic context fingerprints, an editable offline notebook and portable exports.

Its deliberately narrow boundary matters:

- It detects differences in entered data, not whether statements are true.
- It follows declared dependencies, not dependencies inferred from prose.
- A reviewed answer is an acknowledgement of recorded context, not proof of correctness.
- Notebook confirmation is a reproducible content marker, not a signature or permission to execute.
- All demonstration data is synthetic. No real customer decisions or agent conversations are embedded.

A useful next signal would be a real user's saved, non-sensitive example showing a dependency or review workflow the notebook cannot express. Stars alone would not establish that it solves the problem.
